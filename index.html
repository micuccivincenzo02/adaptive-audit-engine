Why

Contracts leak money quietly

Facility-management and service contracts generate monthly invoices that nobody has time to check line by line against the contract terms. A base fee indexed a month early, a penalty clause that goes unapplied, a VAT rate one point off a schedule — each is small on its own, and each compounds over a multi-year contract. Manual reconciliation doesn't scale past a handful of vendors, and most small and mid-size teams don't have a dedicated auditor for it at all.

How

What happens to a document after you drop it in

Three agents, run in sequence, each with one job.

01
Router

Decides the jurisdiction before anything else

Looks at whatever has been uploaded and routes the audit to Italian or Swiss fiscal rules — VAT rates, rounding, currency — before extraction starts.

02
Extractor

Turns a PDF or scan into structured, evidence-backed facts

Claude's Vision API reads each document directly — no local OCR layer — and returns validated line items, totals, dates and clauses, with the original document text kept alongside every extracted value.

03
Critic

Recalculates, cross-checks, and assigns a badge

Every total is recomputed independently with fixed-precision arithmetic and compared against the contract. A finding is marked OK only when there is evidence behind it — never by default.

Evidence

What it actually shows

Running screenshots, using synthetic test invoices and a synthetic contract built to mirror a real facility-management capitolato — no real client data.

From the field

I walked the facility manager at Sunrise, in Switzerland, through a live run of this engine — uploading a real service contract and invoice and watching it surface the same discrepancies a manual line-by-line review would take an afternoon to find.

That conversation is what confirmed the direction: the plain-language explanation behind each finding matters as much as the detection itself, since the person deciding whether to contest an invoice is rarely the person who can read a diff.
